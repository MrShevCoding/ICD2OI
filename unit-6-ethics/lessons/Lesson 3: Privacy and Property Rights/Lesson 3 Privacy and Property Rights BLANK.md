2. **Suppose a 9th grade girl sends an intimate text to her boyfriend. He then puts it on Instagram.  Is it ethical for him to do that?**

In light of these examples, what is privacy?

Have you ever been asked by a cashier for your phone number?  

- Some people give out nothing about themselves.  They don’t share their number, their address, they don’t have Social Media accounts.    
- Others share everything.  Cell phone number, photos of themselves at parties, vacation blogs, etc.

Both people are exercising their right to privacy.  So what is privacy?  Come up with a definition for privacy that applies to both of these kinds of people.

*Privacy is the right to protect yourself against arbitrary, unlawful interference with their private life, home, family, and communications.*

### **Freedom of Property Principle**

True or false:  You may do what you wish with your personal property.

How does this apply to:

* Your home  
* A hockey stick you bought  
* Your cell phone  
* The song you bought from iTunes

Example 1: I buy a hockey stick from Sports World.  It’s mine.  I own it.  Does that mean I can do anything that I want with it?  Can I stop some guy on the street and start wailing away on him with the stick?  And as the police are leading me away, I cry, “But it’s my stick\!”

Example 2: I buy a drum set from Long and McQuade. I carry it into the public library and start going to town on it. When the librarian calls the police can I simply say, “But it’s my drum set?”

**Freedom of Property**

| *You may do what you like with your property AS LONG AS you are not violating the rights of others.* |
