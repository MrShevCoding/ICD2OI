# The Piano Recital Problem SOLUTION

I’m at my son’s piano recital.  He’s the first student to play.  There are 23 other kids scheduled to play, all of them beginners and not very good.  My wife and I decide to slip out after our son is finished so we don’t have to sit through the other pieces, which will likely be boring.  Is it ethical for us to do this?

**Utilitarian analysis**:  What would happen if every parent left as soon as their child was finished playing? 

| Good effects if every parent did this | Bad effects if every parent did this |
| :---- | :---- |
| Every parent has more time to do what they want Less boredom \= greater happiness  Parents can beat the rush at the end and save themselves even more time Parents can focus more on their own kid's performance and remember it better. | Kids at the end feel unappreciated because there's no one left to hear them  Kids who leave early with their parents miss out on hearing other performances, and so miss out on a valuable learning experience. Noise of leaving can distract others and make the children play less well, and detract from the joy of the parents watching their own children play. You might miss some amazing performances (Nouran) You miss the free snacks at the end (Mikael) |

**Weigh the effects**:  Do the good effects seem to outweigh the bad effects?  Or do the bad effects seem to outweigh the good?  In a paragraph of 150-250 words, justify your reasoning.  In other words, why is the losing side not as convincing?

| The good effects only seem to benefit the parents but do not address the needs of the kids, which was the whole point of the recital in the first place.  The main purpose of a music recital is to help kids grow and develop.  They’re meant to give the kids practice in polishing their skills, developing self-confidence, learning to appreciate the musical accomplishments of other children, and learning to perform calmly in front of an audience.  It’s nice if the parents enjoy the show too, but that is secondary and not the main purpose of it.  Thus, arguments that speak to the benefit of the parents at the expense of the kids are not convincing or valid.   By contrast, the kids are harmed in many ways by this kind of selfish behaviour.  They learn that their work is not valued by anyone except their own parents, that it's okay to put your own short-term whims above the good of greater society, and that parents care more about their own time than about kids’ growth.   Therefore, it is unethical for me as parent to leave early because society would suffer if everyone followed my example.  |
